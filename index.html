1. Introduction & Motivation

The Strait of Hormuz constitutes one of the most critical energy chokepoints in the global economy. In early 2026, geopolitical escalation led to a sustained disruption of transit flows equivalent to approximately 20% of global oil supply[100] and a significant share of seaborne LNG trade[101].

This research examines how such a sharp, geopolitically driven energy cost shock propagates through the macroeconomic system and financial markets, with particular attention to differential regional impacts — especially the high energy-import dependence of Japan and other Asian economies.

Methodology & Statistical Transparency

Clear explanation of the methods, the information they use, and why they were chosen for this research.

How the empirical strategy works

We want to answer a simple but important question: when the cost of energy suddenly jumps because of a geopolitical event (the Hormuz disruption), what happens next to inflation, economic activity and financial markets?

To answer it rigorously we combine three complementary tools. Each one looks at the same reality from a different angle:

SVAR

Measures the dynamic transmission of the energy shock to the real economy and asset prices.

GARCH family

Measures how much and in what way market volatility increased during the shock.

Local Projections

Checks whether the SVAR results are robust when we use a more flexible method.

Structural VAR (SVAR) — the core model

What it is (in plain language)

Imagine we have several economic series that move together over time: the price of oil, inflation, industrial production, stock returns and credit spreads. A VAR lets each series depend on its own recent past and on the recent past of the others. The “Structural” part means we add economic reasoning (or an external instrument) so that we can isolate a clean “energy supply shock” instead of a mixture of many things happening at once.

Main components

  • Endogenous variables — the series we model together (energy price, inflation, activity, equity returns, credit spreads).
  • Lags — how many past periods we let influence the present (chosen with information criteria).
  • Identification — the rules that turn statistical residuals into economically meaningful shocks. We use an external instrument: sharp price moves around geopolitical news or sudden drops in Hormuz transit volumes.
  • Impulse responses — the main output: graphs that show how inflation, activity and markets react in the months after the energy shock.

How we actually use it

1. We collect monthly (or higher-frequency) data for the chosen variables.
2. We estimate the reduced-form VAR.
3. We identify the structural energy shock with the external instrument.
4. We compute impulse-response functions with confidence bands.
5. We read the economic story: how large and how persistent is the effect on inflation, growth and asset prices?

Why we chose it for this paper

It is the workhorse method in the modern oil-shock literature. It lets us tell a transparent causal story about transmission while remaining fully replicable with public data.

GARCH family — measuring volatility

What it is (in plain language)

Financial returns are not equally risky every day. Some periods are calm; others are turbulent. GARCH models the variance of returns as a process that depends on recent shocks and on its own recent level. In short: it quantifies “how nervous the market is” at each moment.

Main components / variants

  • GARCH(1,1) — the classic version: today’s volatility depends on yesterday’s volatility and on the size of yesterday’s surprise.
  • EGARCH / GJR-GARCH — allow bad news to increase volatility more than good news (leverage effect), which is common in equity markets.
  • Conditional volatility series — the main output: a daily (or weekly) measure of risk that we can plot around the Hormuz event window.

How we actually use it

1. We take daily returns of equity indices and energy futures.
2. We estimate GARCH, EGARCH and GJR-GARCH.
3. We extract the fitted conditional volatility.
4. We examine whether volatility jumped, how large the jump was, and whether negative returns amplified it during the shock window.

Why we chose it for this paper

The energy shock does not only move price levels; it changes risk. GARCH gives us a disciplined way to measure that change and links the macroeconomic story to the quantitative-finance angle of the research.

Local Projections (Jordà)

What it is (in plain language)

Instead of forcing all dynamics through a single VAR system, Local Projections estimate the response of a variable at each future horizon with a separate regression. They are more flexible and less sensitive to the exact lag structure of a VAR.

Main components

  • Event or shock date — the onset of the sustained Hormuz disruption.
  • Horizon-specific regressions — one regression for the effect after 1 month, another for 2 months, etc.
  • Controls — past values of the variables and other relevant factors.
  • Impulse-response style graphs — easy to compare visually with the SVAR results.

How we actually use it

We treat the beginning of the major disruption as an event, run the sequence of local projections for inflation, activity and asset returns, and plot the resulting path. If this path tells the same economic story as the SVAR, our conclusions gain credibility.

Why we chose it for this paper

It is an excellent robustness tool. It protects us against the criticism that “the results only appear because of a particular VAR specification”.

Why this combination

The three methods are complementary:

  • SVAR gives us the main causal narrative and the dynamic multipliers we need for the macroeconomic story.
  • GARCH adds the risk dimension that matters for financial markets and for the quantitative-finance angle of the work.
  • Local Projections act as an independent check, increasing the transparency and credibility of the results.

Together they allow a dual presentation: a clear, accessible story for the course (surface) and a rigorous, replicable empirical core that can later support master’s-level research (submerged base).
